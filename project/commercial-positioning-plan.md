# Commercial Positioning Plan

Task name: `commercial-positioning-plan-v1`

Date: 2026-05-23

## A. Current Positioning Diagnosis

The site currently communicates as a serious knowledge garden for pricing, value capture, marketing, and commercial decision logic. The homepage frames Dave as a university lecturer focused on pricing strategy and commercial decision logic, and the strongest public assets are the pricing case library, mechanism navigator, EVAC materials, and course structures.

It currently serves three audiences at once: managers and founders, educators, and learners. The content is useful to all three, but the site does not yet give each audience a clear path or next action.

The commercial confusion is that the site has strong advisory evidence but no commercial frame: no About credibility story, no reader-path landing pages, no contact CTA, no service boundaries, and no public explanation of how the case system connects to advisory work. The Brain-only diagnostic layer is strong evidence for internal reasoning quality, but it should remain internal and not be exposed as a public product.

## B. Recommended Positioning Choice

Primary positioning: **Advisory credibility engine**.

This best matches the repository evidence. The pricing cases, mechanism navigator, EVAC framework, governance discipline, and Brain-only advisory tests all point toward a practical credibility layer for people trying to reason through pricing and value-capture decisions.

This is stronger than an academic knowledge garden because the current site already goes beyond teaching notes: it contains applied commercial reasoning, mechanism comparisons, case-based diagnosis, and governance discipline.

This is better than a paid course funnel for the next phase because the reader path, proof, offer scope, and EVAC module completeness are not yet mature enough. A paid course can come later, after credibility and demand signals are clearer.

## C. Target Audiences

### Business managers and founders with pricing problems

They need help framing pricing problems, identifying pricing mechanisms, understanding tradeoffs, and deciding what evidence to test next. Current helpful content includes the Pricing Cases index, Mechanism Navigator, EVAC value-capture logic, and real company pricing examples.

Missing pieces are a clear "I have a pricing problem" path, advisory boundaries, and a low-pressure contact route. Their intended action should be: read a relevant mechanism path, compare one or two cases, then request a diagnostic conversation when a lightweight CTA exists.

### Educators and academic partners

They need reliable cases, structured teaching sequences, conceptual maps, and material that can support workshops, lectures, or applied modules. Current helpful content includes Courses, EVAC, Marketing, the Case Library, and the pricing governance discipline.

Missing pieces are a teaching-partner page, session formats, module outlines, and collaboration boundaries. Their intended action should be: review the course and case materials, then contact Dave about teaching collaboration or a case-based workshop.

### Students and learners

They need a guided learning sequence, accessible starting points, and clear progression from concepts to cases. Current helpful content includes the Marketing course map, EVAC index, Pricing Cases index, and Mechanism Navigator.

Missing pieces are a beginner "start here" route, recommended first cases, and a simple learning sequence. Their intended action should be: follow a pricing learning path and use cases to practice mechanism recognition.

## D. Commercial Promise

Recommended positioning statement:

**Think with Dave helps managers, founders, and educators understand how pricing mechanisms shape revenue, customer behavior, and commercial tradeoffs through real cases, value-capture frameworks, and disciplined diagnostic reasoning.**

This avoids overclaiming AI or automated pricing. It frames the work around pricing mechanisms, value capture, and commercial decision logic.

## E. Reader Paths

### 1. I want to learn pricing

Entry page: existing `cases/pricing/mechanism-navigator.html` for now; later a dedicated pricing start page.

Recommended sequence: Mechanism Navigator -> Pricing Cases index -> selected anchor cases such as AWS, HP, Airbnb, Shopify, Amazon FBA -> EVAC value definition and capture boundary.

Intended next action: continue through a guided learning sequence or subscribe to future case updates if a newsletter layer is later justified.

Missing page or section: "Start Learning Pricing" page with first five recommended cases and a short concept path.

### 2. I have a pricing problem

Entry page: future "Pricing Problem" page or homepage section; interim entry can be the Mechanism Navigator.

Recommended sequence: problem framing prompt -> relevant mechanism cluster -> one or two comparable pricing cases -> EVAC capture-boundary article -> future diagnostic contact CTA.

Intended next action: submit or discuss a pricing problem through one low-risk contact route.

Missing page or section: advisory boundary page or section explaining what Dave can help diagnose, what evidence is needed, and what is not promised.

### 3. I teach or train pricing / commercial decision-making

Entry page: existing `courses/index.html`; later a dedicated teaching page.

Recommended sequence: Courses index -> Marketing course map -> EVAC framework -> Pricing Cases index -> Mechanism Navigator.

Intended next action: contact Dave for a guest session, workshop, teaching collaboration, or module adaptation.

Missing page or section: teaching/collaboration page with sample formats and clear boundaries.

## F. Service / Offer Direction

### Pricing Mechanism Diagnostic Sprint

Target buyer: founder, commercial leader, pricing owner, or product/marketing lead.

Problem solved: unclear pricing architecture, weak packaging logic, discount leakage, value metric uncertainty, or confusion about who carries cost/risk.

Deliverables: problem frame, pricing mechanism map, evidence gaps, option set, pilot recommendation, and risk boundaries.

Evidence still needed: anonymized examples, pilot outcomes, testimonials, or documented advisory cases before making strong public claims.

### Case-based Pricing Workshop

Target buyer: management teams, commercial teams, executive education programs, or business schools.

Problem solved: teams lack shared language for pricing mechanisms and commercial tradeoffs.

Deliverables: curated case sequence, workshop deck, exercises, facilitated discussion, and decision checklist.

Evidence still needed: pilot workshop feedback, clearer session length, learning outcomes, and repeatable workshop structure.

### EVAC / Value Capture Teaching Module

Target buyer: educators, business schools, internal academies, or training partners.

Problem solved: need a structured way to teach value capture, willingness to pay, pricing architecture, and commercial governance.

Deliverables: module outline, readings, selected cases, exercises, and facilitation notes.

Evidence still needed: more complete EVAC module set and validation from teaching or training use.

### Phase 4: Newsletter or case update layer

Possible later files/pages: `newsletter.html` or a small case update section.

## H. What Not To Do Yet

- Do not expose the Brain-only diagnostic architecture directly as a public product.
- Do not describe the advisory layer as automated pricing, AI pricing, or guaranteed pricing optimization.
- Do not create a full sales funnel before positioning, reader paths, and service boundaries are settled.
- Do not turn every page into a CTA.
- Do not promise pricing outcomes without client evidence.
- Do not modify pricing case production contracts, hidden JSON, Layer 1 schemas, or `case-library.js` for commercial positioning.
- Do not make the paid course funnel the next move.

## I. Validation And Decision Criteria

The positioning layer is ready for implementation when:

- The homepage can explain who the site helps and why in one plain-English paragraph.
- An About page can tell a credible story without overclaiming advisory outcomes.
- Three reader paths are defined and map cleanly to existing content.
- One low-risk contact CTA is defined.
- Future service language clearly states scope, deliverables, and boundaries.
- Brain-only diagnostic materials remain internal and are not promoted into public product copy.
- Pricing case contracts, hidden JSON, Layer 1 schemas, and governance rules remain untouched.
- The positioning clearly explains why advisory credibility comes before a paid course funnel.
